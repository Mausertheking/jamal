A student of society, preparing for the law.

Since 2023 I've volunteered at the Mehriban Women and Children Shelter in Baku, which supports survivors of domestic violence. I started by sorting donations and packing support kits for families. Later, with the staff, I helped write material for children about personal boundaries and the difference between an ordinary argument and control.

I research gender norms, too. My lead-authored paper in the American Journal of Student Research looks at how Instagram comments on Azerbaijani news police women's autonomy. And for two years I helped measure the results of Women Forward Azerbaijan, an economic empowerment programme for 420 women in 18 communities.

At school I'm the lead ombudsman, a class representative and a member of the debate club. I want to study law because the problems I keep running into (stalking, coercion, cyberbullying, unequal work) need people who can argue them in court, too.

I also play guitar. I've studied at Music School No. 35 since 2022, I've taught free guitar lessons for three years, and my modeling and performance earnings have paid for instruments at seven rural schools.

Research & Publications

Papers

Three published papers and one independent data study. Most of my work looks at gender, policy and the law; the fourth paper tests a common belief about stock portfolios.

01

Gendered Moral Regulation in Azerbaijani Instagram News Discussions: A Matrix of Domination Analysis

American Journal of Student Research · Vol. 4, Issue 5 · 14 Oct 2026

I led this study and co-wrote the article with Rena Alasgarova. I designed the research question and read 98 Instagram comments across three news stories through intersectional feminist theory, to see how ordinary online discussion polices what women are allowed to choose.

04

Different Stocks, Same Risks? Measuring Hidden Economic Exposures in the S&P 500 with AI-Drafted Scores

PortfolioLab AI · Independent student research · Oct 2026

A 7-page paper measured on real data for all 503 S&P 500 companies. The main finding: across 2,000 random 10-stock portfolios, the correlation between how many sectors a portfolio covers and how many economic forces it depends on was −0.03. Sector spread tells you almost nothing about real diversification. Mentored by Murad, a Stanford University student.
